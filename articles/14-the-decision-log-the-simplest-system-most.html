The Decision Log: The Simplest System Most Teams Skip

Inside an AI-augmented leadership system, the decision log is the cheapest tool with the highest payoff, and almost nobody runs one.

A decision log is a short, running record of what was decided, why, and what would change your mind, and it takes minutes a week to maintain.

Thesis: Teams don't repeat mistakes because they lack talent. They repeat mistakes because they lack memory.

Listen:

Why This Gets Skipped

Decision logs feel like overhead. They don't ship features or close deals, so teams treat them as optional documentation instead of infrastructure.

That's backwards. A decision log isn't paperwork. It's the difference between a team that learns and a team that argues the same point every quarter.

What It Actually Is

A decision log is not a meeting summary. It's a short entry, written the moment a real decision gets made, that captures four things: the decision, the reasoning, the alternative considered, and the condition that would reverse it.

That last piece matters most. Most teams record what they decided. Almost none record what would make them decide differently.

How to Run One

Keep it lightweight or it dies in a week. One shared doc, one line per decision, no approval workflow.

  • Log only decisions that would be expensive to reverse blind
  • Write the entry within 24 hours, while reasoning is still fresh
  • Name the person who owns the call, not just the team
  • Include one line: 'we'll revisit this if X happens'
  • Review the log monthly, not just when something breaks

What It Prevents

Without a log, every disagreement becomes a rerun of the original debate. Nobody remembers what was actually weighed, so the same objections resurface months later as if they're new.

With a log, disagreement becomes cheap to resolve. You point to the entry, check whether the reversal condition has been met, and move on.

The log isn't there to prove you were right. It's there to prove what you knew at the time.

Where It Breaks

Most decision logs fail for the same reasons.

  • Too much detail, so nobody keeps it updated
  • No owner, so entries stay vague and unaccountable
  • No revisit trigger, so it becomes a graveyard instead of a tool
  • Treated as a compliance task instead of a leadership habit

The Real Leverage

A decision log turns judgment into an asset the whole team can draw on, not something locked in one person's head. That's the actual point of an AI-augmented system: fast tools, slower and better-documented human calls.

Skip it, and every hard decision gets made from scratch, every time, forever.
